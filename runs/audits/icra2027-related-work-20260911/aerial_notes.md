# Aerial flexible-cable literature: primary-source comparison

Research checked on 11 September 2026. These are bounded research notes for the paper-framing report, not an experimental protocol or a code change. Facts below are separated from recommendations. No priority claim follows from the absence of an exact match in these searches.

## 1. Shen, Franchi, Gabellieri: closest aerial free-cable control predecessor

**Metadata:** Yaolei Shen, Antonio Franchi, Chiara Gabellieri, *Aerial Robots Carrying Flexible Cables: Dynamic Shape Optimal Control via Spectral Method Model*. IEEE Transactions on Robotics, 2025, vol. 41, pp. 3162–3182. DOI [10.1109/TRO.2025.3562459](https://doi.org/10.1109/TRO.2025.3562459). [Primary arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17565); [full author manuscript v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.17565v2). Journal status and DOI are explicit in the arXiv record; pagination is corroborated by Rapuano et al.'s primary reference [9].

**Source facts (under 180 words):** A single quadrotor carries a free-ended flexible extensible cable. A PDE–ODE model includes the cable force acting on the vehicle; the cable is perfectly flexible, with no bending or torsional torque. Proper orthogonal decomposition supplies a reduced model for cable-shape NMPC. Physical experiments use a 233 g quadrotor, 1 m cable, Crazyflie/Mellinger inner control and OptiTrack cable measurements. Real NMPC operates at 100 Hz with a three-step, 30 ms prediction horizon. Cable elasticity and aerodynamic drag are identified from a fixed-vehicle free-swing recording. Experiments assess regulation, shape tracking and narrow-window passage. Window-crossing references are generated offline by particle-swarm search over a Fourier trajectory, followed by cable-feedback NMPC. The short-horizon controller therefore should not be described as discovering the entire dynamic crossing maneuver online.

**Locators:** §§II-A/B, especially equations (10), (13); §V-E equations (59)–(60); §VI-A/B; §VI-C–E. Real-time figures are in §VI-A, not a general claim about long-horizon nonlinear planning.

**Our analytical distinction:** The proposed whip study should make the tip-strike task, lack of cable feedback during execution, and repeated refinement of the command-to-tip predictor explicit. A generic “aerial flexible-cable dynamics and control” contribution would overlap heavily. Our effective loaded-vehicle cascade is less mechanically complete in one respect: it does not exchange cable reaction force with the UAV. It should be defended by prospective prediction accuracy over the stated task envelope, not called a more physically faithful coupled model. The rotated attachment offset is a useful implementation detail, not a standalone contribution.

## 2. Rapuano et al.: recent ICRA continuum and hybrid manipulation

**Metadata:** Antonio Rapuano, Yaolei Shen, Federico Califano, Chiara Gabellieri, Antonio Franchi, *Nonlinear Predictive Control of the Continuum and Hybrid Dynamics of a Suspended Deformable Cable for Aerial Pick and Place*. ICRA 2026, acceptance and journal reference explicit in [primary arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17199), submitted 19 February 2026. [Full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17199v1). No verified IEEE proceedings DOI was needed for this comparison.

**Source facts (under 180 words):** Extends continuum/POD aerial cable modeling to payload attachment and detachment. The reduced model preserves the boundary-point dynamics and reduces the interior; separate free-tip and payload bases permit hybrid transitions. Hybrid NMPC tracks references and predicts attachment impacts, using hybrid iLQR variants. Reported evaluation is numerical: high-dimensional finite-difference dynamics provide the reference model, with tests of reduced-model error, tracking, hybrid transitions and constrained pick-and-place planning. The simulated controller uses 40 Hz sampling and 0.8 s lookahead. Performance depends on solver choice: the simplified real-time-iteration variant fails the more complex obstacle task while full hybrid iLQR completes it. This paper does not provide real-flight repeated model-refinement or free-tip striking evidence.

**Locators:** §III-B/C; §IV numerical experiments, especially §IV-A and Table III. Abstract explicitly says simulation results. Avoid calling the model-reduction snapshot procedure physical system identification.

**Our analytical distinction:** This is a strong citation for dynamic shape-aware aerial manipulation and a warning against implying that a massless pendulum is the only predecessor. The paper's algorithmic contribution is broader than our task-specific identification recipe. Our opportunity is a carefully measured physical system result for impulsive free-tip targeting, with prospective model-update evidence. A comparative claim about MPC speed would be meaningless across their reduced model, closed-loop tracking task and our offline full-maneuver search.

## 3. Zhai et al.: close high-agility aerial sim-to-real precedent

**Metadata:** Yifan Zhai, Elia Raimondi, Yunfan Ren, Ismail Geles, Yannick Armati, Jiaxu Xing, Davide Scaramuzza, *Learning to Throw: Agile and Accurate Cable-Suspended Payload Delivery with a Quadrotor*. [arXiv:2606.27603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27603), submitted 25 June 2026; **preprint**, with no accepted venue stated in the verified record. [Full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27603v1).

**Source facts (under 180 words):** An identified standalone quadrotor model supplies wrench commands to a PhysX articulated rope/payload simulator, which returns the vehicle state and includes reaction forces. PPO produces thrust/body-rate/release actions at 50 Hz; coupled simulation runs at 500 Hz. Hardware uses a 210 g quadrotor, 33 cm tether and tennis-ball payload. The payload/tether is released for a ballistic landing. Real zero-shot tests include several targets and simulator-component ablations. The main trajectory-optimization-plus-MPC baseline is evaluated in simulation, whereas PPO is evaluated on hardware; the comparison is not a matched physical planner trial. The visual-observation variant uses hardware-in-the-loop projected keypoints, avoiding an actual object-detection module. Its identification is performed once on the standalone vehicle, not repeated task-data updates of the vehicle and cable models.

**Locators:** §§III-A/B/C; Table I; §IV-A and Table III (explicit baseline setting); §IV-B and Tables V (ablations); §IV-C (HIL vision qualification); §V (scope and limitations).

**Our analytical distinction:** “Combining a learned or identified UAV simulator with flexible-rope physics for agile sim-to-real manipulation” is already close prior art. Our potential contribution is a reusable command-conditioned model improved between retained-cable strikes, and evidence that the update changes prospective planning performance. A free-ended, retained distributed cable is a materially different task from a tethered dominant payload with release, but that difference alone does not establish scientific value. Show why predicting the attachment response and distributed transient deformation is necessary in our experiment. Do not describe our PVA pipeline as state-feedback-equivalent to this policy. Their paper's claim about a plan/track bottleneck makes our learned command-to-executed-motion model relevant, but it is a hypothesis requiring evidence, not an automatic rebuttal.

## 4. Gabellieri et al.: real identification and model validation already exist

**Metadata:** Chiara Gabellieri, Lars Teeuwen, Yaolei Shen, Antonio Franchi, *Manipulation of Elasto-Flexible Cables with Single or Multiple UAVs*. IROS 2025. DOI [10.1109/IROS60139.2025.11246978](https://doi.org/10.1109/IROS60139.2025.11246978). [Primary arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04304); [full revised author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04304v2). Initial submission 6 March 2025; v2 dated 13 October 2025. The current arXiv metadata explicitly lists IROS 2025.

**Source facts (under 180 words):** Derives flat outputs for classes of single-/multi-UAV systems with spring-linked lumped cable masses, including a hanging free tip. Real identification and validation concern a two-UAV cable. Six physical parameters are estimated by simulation-error optimization with a homotopy construction, using manually excited cable endpoints as measured inputs. Subsequent UAV experiments use precomputed trajectories across several motions and velocities. Cable-only prediction is assessed by replaying measured UAV endpoints, deliberately excluding UAV tracking error. A further controller recomputes UAV references using cable-output integral feedback. The paper explicitly distinguishes identification data and robot validation, while noting that no internal training/validation split was made within its parameter-estimation dataset. Its physical cable-model validation therefore supports the usefulness of measured-boundary diagnostics, but does not validate our complete command-to-tip cascade.

**Locators:** §II; §III; §V-A, equations (12), Tables I/II; §V-B; §VI. The identification versus whole-chain distinction is explicit in §V-A.

**Our analytical distinction:** Neither fitting cable parameters from real trajectories nor evaluating them on new robot motions is new by itself. Our claim should require the complete executable-command prediction, adaptation of both effective UAV and cable response, and a new prospective strike after replanning. Retain measured-attachment prediction as a diagnostic, but do not make it the headline success measure of sim-to-real planning.

## Cross-paper implications for framing and evidence

1. **Name the task precisely.** “Dynamic free-tip targeting with a UAV-carried cable” identifies the setting without claiming general aerial deformable manipulation. “Whipping” needs operationally defined evidence: fast free-tip motion, a transient deformation sequence, tip-target error, and UAV motion at approach. A cartoon wave, a high reward or a contact gate alone does not establish energy transfer.
2. **Name the model honestly.** The present model is a calibrated effective UAV-response model cascaded into a cable dynamics predictor. It is not a mechanically bidirectionally coupled quadrotor–cable simulator. Loaded-UAV fitting may absorb average cable effects, but whether this transfers to new command/cable states is an empirical question.
3. **The central test is prospective.** Freeze an updated model before generating the next CSV, retain its original forecast, and compare that forecast against new flights. A retrospectively improved fit on the same commands is valuable identification evidence but does not establish better task achievement.
4. **Separate three errors.** UAV command-to-motion error; cable error with measured attachment; complete command-to-tip error. The third determines whether the planner receives an adequate model. The first two diagnose where an update works or fails. Their improvements can coexist with a combined regression.
5. **Use matched baselines where a causal claim requires them.** The strongest affordable baseline is a frozen initial model given the same planning objective, seed library and compute budget. Evaluate both model generations on identical held-out commands, then compare separately generated commands in fresh physical trials. Do not compare own-command M0/M1/M2 error as a pure model-only effect when rewards or search procedures changed.
6. **Show one meaningful generalization axis if feasible.** A second target or different whip amplitude planned with the same frozen updated model, without target-specific fitting, helps distinguish a reusable model from one-trajectory correction. This is more useful than adding an unrelated task demonstration with no clear hypothesis.
7. **Control the initial-condition explanation.** Report prelaunch position and cable motion; freeze a reasonable launch procedure. Nominal-start forecast error and measured-start diagnostic error answer different questions. Do not erase deployment error by retrospectively initializing from future observations or aligning trajectories to maximize agreement.
8. **Keep comparison claims narrow.** The reviewed aerial papers contain real feedback controllers, identified models, distributed dynamics, and agile RL transfer. None supports a blanket assertion that existing work only suppresses swing or only models cables as massless rigid links. Our paper can make a sound systems contribution without claiming a new identifier or universal planner superiority.

## Search boundary

Primary arXiv records, full HTML author manuscripts and referenced publication metadata were checked. Search queries combined aerial/quadrotor, flexible cable, free tip, whip, dynamic manipulation, identification and target strike. No exact UAV free-tip sim–real–sim repeated-refit strike paper was established by this bounded search. That is a search result, not proof of being first. Two ground-robot leads passed to the whip cluster were IRP and the 2026 *DeformX* co-simulation paper; the latter's open-loop replay and high-speed striking demonstration are particularly important for avoiding an overly broad claim.
