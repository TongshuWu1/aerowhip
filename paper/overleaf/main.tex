\begin{abstract}
Flexible cables provide a lightweight means for aerial robots to
reach and interact with distant targets. However, directing a
cable's free tip accurately during a rapid whipping motion is
difficult because vehicle tracking errors and uncertain cable
dynamics alter its trajectory. This paper presents AeroWhip,
a framework for targeted whipping with a quadrotor and a free-ended
cable. Its command-to-tip predictor combines fitted vehicle
response, attachment geometry, and differentiable rod dynamics
with learned acceleration corrections. Initial planning searches
vehicle commands in simulation to select a tip trajectory and
timing. Between flight batches, we refine the predictor from
measured motion and use it to correct commands toward this fixed
tip reference. Planning and correction are performed offline;
the onboard controller tracks the prescribed commands without
online cable feedback. In physical experiments, one round of
model refinement and command correction reduces mean targeting
error by 54\% relative to the initial model and command. Using the
latest model, the system hits the target in 17 of 20 runs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Aerial robots use ropes and cables to transport suspended
payloads and grasp objects~\cite{sreenath2013trajectory,hitch}.
We study targeted aerial whipping, in which a quadrotor drives
one end of a hanging cable to direct its free tip toward a
target (Fig.~\ref{fig:physical_strike}). Producing the desired tip
motion requires accounting for uncertainties in both vehicle
response and cable dynamics.

Whipping and dynamic cable manipulation have been studied through
model-based optimization with robotic
arms~\cite{zimmermann2021dynamic}, movement primitives in a
simulated human arm~\cite{nah2023learning}, and learning-based
control~\cite{wang2024learning}. In aerial whipping, the quadrotor
must also maintain flight while driving the cable. Vehicle
translation, rotation, and tracking errors determine the motion
of its offset attachment during rapid
flight~\cite{torrente2021data}. Command design must therefore
account for how this attachment motion drives cable deformation
and free-tip motion.

Aerial cable control has addressed suspended-payload trajectory
generation~\cite{sreenath2013trajectory}, flexible-cable
dynamics~\cite{8308004}, agile flight~\cite{cao2026flare}, and
adaptation to changes in flight dynamics~\cite{belkhale2021model}.
For a single quadrotor carrying a free-ended cable, Shen
et al.~\cite{shen2025aerial} combine parameter identification with
online predictive control of cable shape. Other work combines
identification from measured endpoint motion with cable
manipulation using multiple UAVs~\cite{gabellieri2025manipulation}.

Discrete elastic rod models describe cable deformation through
distributed bending and twisting~\cite{bergou2008discrete}.
DEFORM combines differentiable rod mechanics with learning for
cable prediction, tracking, and
manipulation~\cite{pmlr-v270-chen25d}. Related studies investigate
physical parameter identification~\cite{chen2025accurate} and
structured dynamics learning from limited
measurements~\cite{mamedov2025learning}.

Repeated executions can improve dynamic manipulation by updating
commands or the models used to generate them. Iterative Residual
Policy refines actions from previous
executions~\cite{chi2024iterative}, while task-level iterative
learning control uses measured rope-state errors to refine robot
commands~\cite{suresh2026learning}. Model-based iterative learning
also improves quadrotor tracking by updating feedforward commands
across repetitions~\cite{schoellig2012optimization}.
Model refinement is used in Real2Sim2Real to improve simulation
and subsequent casting actions~\cite{lim2022real2sim2real}, and
Wiggle and Go identifies rope properties through a probing motion
before planning dynamic tasks~\cite{jakobsson2026wiggle}.
Sampling-based planning has also been combined with repeated
fitting of learned dynamics~\cite{williams2017information}.

We present \emph{AeroWhip}, a framework for targeted aerial
whipping that combines iterative model refinement with command
correction toward a fixed tip reference. Its predictor links a
fitted vehicle response and attachment geometry to a reduced
differentiable discrete elastic rod (DDER) model with learned
acceleration corrections. An optimizer inspired by model
predictive path integral (MPPI)
control~\cite{williams2017model} searches vehicle commands in
simulation to select a desired tip trajectory and timing.

Between flight batches, measurements refine the vehicle and
cable models. Local command correction then optimizes commands
to track the selected tip trajectory under the updated model.
The reference trajectory and timing remain fixed as the commands
change. When the
selected tip trajectory remains suitable and achievable, command
correction avoids another MPPI search. Physical experiments
evaluate target approach and prediction accuracy and demonstrate
repeated contact with the target.

\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem_statement}

In this work, we address the problem of directing the free tip
of a cable toward a target using a quadrotor. The cable has
length $L>0$, with one end attached at a fixed point on the
vehicle and the other free. The maneuver starts from nominal
hover with the cable hanging at rest.

Let $t=0$ denote maneuver onset and $T>0$ the targeting horizon.
The stationary target is located at
$\mathbf p_{\mathrm{tar}}\in\mathbb R^3$. A prescribed horizontal
unit vector $\mathbf e\in\mathbb R^3$ defines the preferred
direction of tip motion independently of target position.
Position, velocity, and acceleration commands are computed
offline with fixed desired yaw and tracked by the onboard
controller without cable feedback.

Initial planning optimizes target approach and whipping motion
subject to command limits and feasibility checks in simulation
(Section~\ref{subsec:offline_planning}).

Figure~\ref{fig:problem_whipping} illustrates the motion pattern
favored by initial planning: a forward and upward pull followed
by vehicle retraction. The schematic depicts a bend propagating
toward the free end as the cable straightens and the tip
approaches the target, consistent with travelling-deformation
descriptions of whipping~\cite{MCMILLEN2003192}. We describe this
motion through cable shape and tip kinematics.

Let $\mathbf r(s,t)\in\mathbb R^3$ denote the world-frame position
of a point at distance $sL$ along the unstretched cable, measured
from the attachment. The coordinate $s\in[0,1]$ identifies the same
point throughout the motion, and $\mathbf r(\cdot,t)$ is the
centerline at time $t$.
The attachment and tip positions are
$\mathbf p_a(t)=\mathbf r(0,t)$ and
$\mathbf r_{\mathrm{tip}}(t)=\mathbf r(1,t)$, respectively,
with tip velocity
$\mathbf v_{\mathrm{tip}}(t)=\dot{\mathbf r}_{\mathrm{tip}}(t)$.
Tip velocity describes approach speed and alignment with
$\mathbf e$.

Target proximity is quantified by the minimum
three-dimensional tip-to-target distance:
\begin{equation}
d_{\min}
=\min_{t\in[0,T]}
\|\mathbf r_{\mathrm{tip}}(t)-\mathbf p_{\mathrm{tar}}\|_2.
\label{eq:target_distance}
\end{equation}

For shape comparison, predicted and reference centerlines are
sampled at $M\geq3$ uniformly spaced coordinates
$s_j=j/(M-1)$, $j=0,\ldots,M-1$.
These samples need not coincide with measurement markers.
Throughout, segment directions are endpoint differences normalized
by their Euclidean norm and oriented from attachment toward tip.
For distinct neighboring shape samples, denote these directions by
$\mathbf d_j(t)$, $j=0,\ldots,M-2$.
The sampled positions and directions define the planner's
reference-shape comparison.

\subsection{DDER Cable Model}
\label{subsec:dder_model}

We use a reduced DDER model based on the rod mechanics of
Bergou et al.~\cite{bergou2008discrete}. The cable is assumed to
pivot freely at the attachment, with uniform material properties,
negligible axial extension, a straight and untwisted rest
configuration, and isotropic bending stiffness.

The centerline is discretized using $N\geq3$ points that move
with the cable (Fig.~\ref{fig:problem_dder}), with node positions
$\mathbf r_i(t)\in\mathbb R^3$ and velocities
$\mathbf v_i(t)\in\mathbb R^3$, $i=0,\ldots,N-1$.
The attachment and free-tip nodes satisfy
$\mathbf r_0=\mathbf p_a$ and
$\mathbf r_{N-1}=\mathbf r_{\mathrm{tip}}$, respectively.

Segment $i$ connects nodes $i$ and $i+1$ and has fixed length
$\ell_i>0$. Inextensibility imposes
\[
\|\mathbf r_{i+1}-\mathbf r_i\|_2=\ell_i,
\qquad
\sum_{i=0}^{N-2}\ell_i=L.
\]
Linear interpolation between nodes defines $\mathbf r(s,t)$ and
the shape samples above. The node count $N$ and shape-sample
count $M$ need not be equal.

For the straight, isotropic rod, we assume cross-sectional
orientations remain at elastic equilibrium with no imposed end
twist or twisting moment, giving zero material
twist~\cite{bergou2008discrete}. The model therefore omits
independent twist coordinates and torsional energy while
retaining three-dimensional centerline dynamics.

Let $\mathbf t_i$, $i=0,\ldots,N-2$, be the
unit direction from node $i$ to node $i+1$, using the same
convention as $\mathbf d_j$ for shape samples.
For each interior node $i=1,\ldots,N-2$, let
$\bar\ell_i=(\ell_{i-1}+\ell_i)/2$ be the average rest length
of its adjacent segments.
The discrete curvature binormal and bending energy are
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol\kappa_i
=\frac{2\,\mathbf t_{i-1}\times\mathbf t_i}
{1+\mathbf t_{i-1}^\top\mathbf t_i},
\qquad
E_b=\frac{EI}{2}
\sum_{i=1}^{N-2}\frac{\|\boldsymbol\kappa_i\|_2^2}{\bar\ell_i},
\label{eq:bending_energy}
\end{equation}
where $EI>0$ is the bending stiffness and
$\boldsymbol\kappa_i$ is the dimensionless curvature binormal.

The ideal bending force at node $i$ is
$\mathbf f_{b,i}=-\partial E_b/\partial\mathbf r_i$.
Numerical evaluation uses a positive denominator floor near
antiparallel tangents.
Time integration, constraint enforcement, and learned
acceleration corrections are described in
Section~\ref{subsec:prediction_model}.

The main problem addressed in this work is the following.

\noindent\textbf{Problem 1} (Aerial Cable Whipping).
Given an initial hovering configuration, target
$\mathbf p_{\mathrm{tar}}$, preferred strike direction $\mathbf e$,
and horizon $T$, generate admissible quadrotor commands to reduce
tip-to-target separation within $[0,T]$ through a whipping motion.

To solve this problem, we consider three subproblems.

\noindent\textbf{Subproblem 1} (Command-to-Tip Prediction).
Predict cable motion from desired quadrotor commands,
accounting for vehicle response, attachment geometry, and cable
dynamics.

\noindent\textbf{Subproblem 2} (Offline Trajectory Optimization).
Select a desired tip trajectory and timing by optimizing
vehicle commands in simulation. After model refinement, correct
the commands to track the same reference.

\noindent\textbf{Subproblem 3} (Model Refinement).
Refine the vehicle and cable models using recorded commands
and measured motion to improve command-to-tip prediction.

\section{AeroWhip Framework}
\label{sec:aerowhip_framework}

We combine the cable model with a fitted vehicle response to
predict motion from commands. The resulting predictor is held
fixed during trajectory optimization and refined between flight
batches.

\subsection{Quadrotor and Cable Prediction Model}
\label{subsec:prediction_model}

The predictor maps commands to vehicle motion and drives the cable
with the resulting attachment trajectory. Its fitted vehicle
response approximates cable-loading effects under the observed
conditions without explicit cable-force feedback. States below
denote predictions unless marked as measured.

The inputs are desired position $\mathbf p_d$, velocity
$\mathbf v_d$, acceleration $\mathbf a_d$, and yaw $\psi_d$,
held constant between command updates. Let $\mathbf p_q$
and $\mathbf v_q$ denote the position and velocity of the
vehicle's tracked origin, and let $\mathbf R\in\mathrm{SO}(3)$
map vectors from the vehicle's body-fixed tracking frame to
the world frame.
We approximate the closed-loop translational response by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf a_{\mathrm{nom}}(t)
={}&\mathbf K_p[\mathbf p_d(t-\tau_d)-\mathbf p_q(t)]\\
&+\mathbf K_v[\mathbf v_d(t-\tau_d)-\mathbf v_q(t)]\\
&+\mathbf K_a\mathbf a_d(t-\tau_d)+\mathbf b_q,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:quadrotor_response}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_d\geq0$ is an effective command delay and
$\mathbf b_q$ is a constant acceleration bias representing hover
compensation. The fitted diagonal gains $\mathbf K_p$,
$\mathbf K_v$, and $\mathbf K_a$ each use one horizontal and one vertical
coefficient. Translation satisfies
$\dot{\mathbf p}_q=\mathbf v_q$ and
$\dot{\mathbf v}_q=\mathbf a_{\mathrm{nom}}+\Delta\mathbf a_q$,
where $\Delta\mathbf a_q$ is a learned acceleration correction.

Vehicle attitude determines the rotation of the offset attachment.
We construct a commanded rotation $\mathbf R_c$ with its third
axis parallel to
$\mathbf S\mathbf a_{\mathrm{nom}}+g\mathbf e_3$,
where $g>0$ is gravitational acceleration,
$\mathbf e_3=(0,0,1)^\top$ is the upward world direction,
and $\mathbf S$ is a fitted positive diagonal matrix with
dimensionless horizontal and vertical scales.
The first axis is the normalized projection of the horizontal
heading $(\cos\psi_d,\sin\psi_d,0)^\top$ onto the plane normal
to the third axis; the second completes a right-handed frame.
The construction requires nonzero specific force and a nonparallel
heading.

A fixed alignment $\mathbf Q\in\mathrm{SO}(3)$ maps the tracking
frame to the vehicle-axis convention used for $\mathbf R_c$.
Let $\mathbf e_R$ be the principal rotation vector of
$\mathbf R^\top\mathbf R_c\mathbf Q$, expressed in the tracking frame.
The attitude response is
\begin{equation}
\dot{\mathbf R}=\mathbf R[\boldsymbol\omega]_\times,
\qquad
\dot{\boldsymbol\omega}=\frac{\mathbf e_R}{\tau_R^2}
-\frac{2\boldsymbol\omega}{\tau_R},
\label{eq:attitude_response}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol\omega$ is tracking-frame angular velocity,
$\tau_R>0$ is a fitted response timescale, and
$[\boldsymbol\omega]_\times$ denotes the cross-product matrix.
The vehicle correction $\Delta\mathbf a_q$ affects attitude
through the evolving tracking errors in $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{nom}}$.

For a fixed tracking-frame offset $\boldsymbol\rho_a$
from the tracked origin to the attachment, the attachment
position is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf p_a=\mathbf p_q+\mathbf R\boldsymbol\rho_a.
\label{eq:attachment_mapping}
\end{equation}
The attachment trajectory prescribes the cable root,
$\mathbf r_0=\mathbf p_a$.

For each free node $i=1,\ldots,N-1$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
m_i\dot{\mathbf v}_i
={}&\mathbf f_{b,i}
   +\mathbf f_{\mathrm{bd},i}
   +\mathbf f_{\mathrm{con},i}\\
&-m_i\gamma_c\mathbf v_i
 -m_i g\mathbf e_3
 +m_i\Delta\mathbf a_{c,i},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:driven_cable_dynamics}
\end{equation}
with $\dot{\mathbf r}_i=\mathbf v_i$.
Here $m_i$ includes cable and marker mass,
$\mathbf f_{b,i}$ is the bending force from~\eqref{eq:bending_energy}, and
$\mathbf f_{\mathrm{bd},i}$ is the bending-damping force.
The reaction $\mathbf f_{\mathrm{con},i}$ enforces fixed
segment lengths, $\gamma_c\geq0$ is the linear-drag rate,
and $\Delta\mathbf a_{c,i}$ is a learned acceleration correction.

Bending damping penalizes a regularized curvature rate that
approximately removes local rigid rotation. At each interior
node, define
\[
\mathbf n_i=\mathbf t_{i-1}\times\mathbf t_i,
\qquad
c_i=\mathbf t_{i-1}^\top\mathbf t_i.
\]
A dimensionless $\varepsilon>0$ regularizes nearly aligned tangents.
Using $\boldsymbol\kappa_i$ from~\eqref{eq:bending_energy},
define the local angular-velocity estimate and bending rate
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\boldsymbol\omega_i^{\mathrm{loc}}
&=\tfrac12(\mathbf I-\mathbf t_{i-1}\mathbf t_{i-1}^\top)
(\mathbf t_{i-1}\times\dot{\mathbf t}_{i-1}
+\mathbf t_i\times\dot{\mathbf t}_i),\\
\dot{\boldsymbol\kappa}_{i,\mathrm{rel}}
&=\frac{\varepsilon\bigl(\dot{\boldsymbol\kappa}_i
-\boldsymbol\omega_i^{\mathrm{loc}}\times\boldsymbol\kappa_i\bigr)
-2\mathbf n_i\dot c_i/(1+c_i)}
{\|\mathbf n_i\|_2^2+\varepsilon}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:regularized_bending_rate}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathbf I$ is the $3\times3$ identity matrix, and time
derivatives are induced by node velocities.

The dissipation function gives the bending-damping force at node $i$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D_b=\frac{C_b}{2}
\sum_{i=1}^{N-2}\frac{\|\dot{\boldsymbol\kappa}_{i,\mathrm{rel}}\|_2^2}{\bar\ell_i},
\qquad
\mathbf f_{\mathrm{bd},i}=-\frac{\partial\mathcal D_b}{\partial\mathbf v_i},
\label{eq:bending_damping}
\end{equation}
where $C_b\geq0$ is the bending-damping coefficient.
The cable state is advanced with implicit bending damping and
mass-weighted position and velocity projections for the
inextensibility constraints.

Two multilayer perceptrons provide the learned acceleration
corrections. The vehicle network takes the position and velocity
tracking errors in~\eqref{eq:quadrotor_response}, delayed desired
acceleration, predicted velocity, and bias $\mathbf b_q$,
and outputs $\Delta\mathbf a_q$.
The cable network takes all node positions and velocities
relative to the attachment,
$\mathbf r_i-\mathbf r_0$ and $\mathbf v_i-\mathbf v_0$,
together with attachment velocity $\mathbf v_0$.
It jointly outputs $\Delta\mathbf a_{c,i}$ for all free nodes;
the prescribed root receives no correction.

Both networks use world-frame components and fixed input scaling.
Each bounds its output components by a network-specific
$a_{\max}>0$ using a scaled hyperbolic tangent. A factor that
increases with predicted and commanded motion suppresses the
vehicle correction at rest. Weights remain fixed during rollouts
and are fitted in Section~\ref{subsec:model_refinement}.

Preflight prediction starts from nominal hover with zero yaw:
the cable hangs
straight, all velocities and $\mathbf b_q$ are zero, and
$\mathbf R(0)=\mathbf Q=\mathbf I$. Before $t=0$, commands hold
the initial position with zero velocity and acceleration.
Fitting and postflight prediction instead initialize states,
hover compensation, and alignment from preceding measurements.

\subsection{Offline Trajectory Optimization}
\label{subsec:offline_planning}

Initial planning and subsequent command correction use the same
B-spline command representation and admissibility constraints.
Planning scores target approach and whipping motion; correction
minimizes deviations from the retained reference. Physical
parameters and network weights remain fixed during each
optimization.

We represent the desired vehicle position by a smooth command spline
using a fixed clamped quintic B-spline basis:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf p_s(t;\boldsymbol\theta)
=\sum_{j=0}^{n_c-1}B_j(t)\mathbf p_j^{\mathrm{ctrl}},
\qquad t\in[0,T].
\label{eq:spline_reference}
\end{equation}
Here $B_j$ are the basis functions and $\mathbf p_j^{\mathrm{ctrl}}\in\mathbb R^3$
are the $n_c\geq6$ control points. The first three are fixed at
$\mathbf p_q(0)$, giving the initial position and zero initial
velocity and acceleration.
The remaining control points form
$\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^{3(n_c-3)}$.
Desired position, velocity, and acceleration are sampled from
$\mathbf p_s$, $\dot{\mathbf p}_s$, and $\ddot{\mathbf p}_s$,
respectively, and held between command updates. Desired yaw
remains constant. The spline end is free to support follow-through.

A selected command from earlier MPPI development initializes the
spline through a constrained least-squares fit of its desired
positions. The seed is reevaluated under the current model and
target. Admissible candidates satisfy bounds on spline jerk,
command speed and height, and
the magnitude, tilt, and vertical component of
$\mathbf a_d+g\mathbf e_3$. Predicted vehicle height, cable
ground clearance, and numerical validity are also checked.

For initial whip design, the optimizer maximizes
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
J(\boldsymbol\theta)
={}&w_{\mathrm{whip}}S_{\mathrm{tar}}S_{\mathrm{whip}}
+w_{\mathrm{enc}}S_{\mathrm{enc}}+w_{\mathrm{ext}}S_{\mathrm{ext}}\\
&-w_{\mathrm{miss}}(1-S_{\mathrm{tar}})
-C_{\mathrm{motion}}-C_{\mathrm{exit}},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:planning_score}
\end{equation}
where $S_{\mathrm{tar}}$ measures target proximity and $S_{\mathrm{whip}}$
measures reference-shape agreement. The scores $S_{\mathrm{enc}}$
and $S_{\mathrm{ext}}$ favor vehicle retraction and directed tip
extension, while $C_{\mathrm{motion}}$ and $C_{\mathrm{exit}}$
penalize vehicle motion and recovery handover conditions.
The score weights are positive; all terms use the candidate rollout.

\noindent\emph{Target approach and directed motion.}
A sphere of radius $r_{\mathrm{tar}}>0$ centered at
$\mathbf p_{\mathrm{tar}}$ defines the planning tolerance region.
The encounter time $t_e$ is the first entry by any cable node
over $[0,T]$; if none enters, it is the time of closest tip
approach. Entry is evaluated along interpolated node trajectories;
the tolerance sphere does not exert collision forces. The proximity
score is $S_{\mathrm{tar}}=\exp[-\|\mathbf r_{\mathrm{tip}}(t_e)
-\mathbf p_{\mathrm{tar}}\|_2^2/\sigma_p^2]$, where $\sigma_p>0$. This encounter-based distance differs from the
physical minimum-distance metric in~\eqref{eq:target_distance}.

At $t_e$, let $v_{\mathrm{tip},\parallel}=\mathbf e^\top\mathbf v_{\mathrm{tip}}$
and $v_{q,\parallel}=\mathbf e^\top\mathbf v_q$ denote the forward
tip and vehicle velocities. Let $b_e$ be the vehicle's retraction
from its greatest preceding forward position, and let
$\ell_e=\mathbf e^\top(\mathbf r_{\mathrm{tip}}-\mathbf p_a)$
be the forward attachment-to-tip extension. The indicator $\chi$
is one when no node enters the sphere or when the tip enters
strictly before every other node, and zero otherwise.
Define $\operatorname{clip}(z)=\min(1,\max(0,z))$ and
$h(z)=h_{\min}+(1-h_{\min})\operatorname{clip}(z)$, with a fixed
floor $0<h_{\min}<1$. Then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
S_{\mathrm{enc}}
&=S_{\mathrm{tar}}\chi\,h(v_{\mathrm{tip},\parallel}/v_{\mathrm{tip},s})
h(-v_{q,\parallel}/v_{q,s})h(b_e/b_s),\\
S_{\mathrm{ext}}
&=S_{\mathrm{tar}}\chi\,\operatorname{clip}(v_{\mathrm{tip},\parallel}/v_{\mathrm{tip},s})
\operatorname{clip}(\ell_e/L)\\
&\quad\times\operatorname{clip}\!\bigl(
v_{\mathrm{tip},\parallel}/\|\mathbf v_{\mathrm{tip}}\|_2\bigr)^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:directed_motion_scores}
\end{equation}
Positive scales $v_{\mathrm{tip},s}$, $v_{q,s}$, and $b_s$ set
saturation levels. The alignment factor is zero at zero tip speed;
the floor in $h$ encourages but does not require vehicle reversal.

\noindent\emph{Whipping-shape agreement.}
The shape prior, denoted by superscript $\mathrm{ref}$, is an
earlier MPPI-generated cable trajectory selected during development
for its bending and extension sequence. It supplies a desired
motion pattern, while the seed supplies initial commands; MPPI
refines the commands for the current model and target.
Section~\ref{subsec:experimental_setup} specifies their development
geometry. The resulting M0 forecast supplies the separate, fixed
trajectory reference for command correction.

Normalize the sampled positions as
$\bar{\mathbf r}_j(t)=[\mathbf r(s_j,t)-\mathbf p_a(t)]/L$,
removing attachment translation and length scale while retaining
world orientation. Apply the same normalization to the reference
using its own attachment and length.

We align encounter times and allow limited intermediate retiming.
For $K\geq2$ phases $u_k=k/(K-1)$, $k=0,\ldots,K-1$,
define paired times $t_k^{\mathrm{ref}}=t_e^{\mathrm{ref}}u_k$
and $t_k^\gamma=t_eu_k^\gamma$. Here $t_e^{\mathrm{ref}}>0$ is the
reference encounter time, and $\gamma$ belongs to a fixed finite
set $\Gamma$ of positive values containing $1$.
The shape score is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
S_{\mathrm{whip}}&=\max_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\exp\Bigl[
-\frac{\bigl\langle\|\bar{\mathbf r}_j(t_k^\gamma)
-\bar{\mathbf r}_j^{\mathrm{ref}}(t_k^{\mathrm{ref}})\|_2^2\bigr\rangle_{j,k}}{\sigma_r^2}\\
&\quad-\frac{\bigl\langle\|\mathbf d_j(t_k^\gamma)
-\mathbf d_j^{\mathrm{ref}}(t_k^{\mathrm{ref}})\|_2^2\bigr\rangle_{j,k}}{\sigma_d^2}
-\beta|\log\gamma|\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:whip_similarity}
\end{equation}
The brackets average over phases and corresponding shape
samples: $M$ positions or $M-1$ unit directions. Dimensionless scales
$\sigma_r,\sigma_d>0$ set shape tolerance, and $\beta\geq0$
penalizes retiming. We set $S_{\mathrm{whip}}=0$ if $t_e=0$.
The score compares deformation histories; directed-motion rewards
and command constraints govern physical timing.

The cost $C_{\mathrm{motion}}$ integrates weighted squares of
positive forward and lateral vehicle displacement from launch and
spline jerk normalized by its bounds, until tip entry or $T$. The handover cost
$C_{\mathrm{exit}}$ penalizes command speed, upward motion, and
acceleration along the velocity before recovery. Infeasible
candidates receive $J=-\infty$.

\noindent\emph{Initial planning by sampling.}
The offline search uses exponential trajectory
weighting~\cite{williams2017model}. Around each proposal mean
$\boldsymbol\mu$, it samples
$\boldsymbol\theta^{(b)}=\boldsymbol\mu+\boldsymbol\epsilon^{(b)}$
from a mixture of correlated Gaussian perturbations. Correlation
encourages coherent changes in neighboring control points.

Let $\mathcal V$ index a proposal's feasible random samples and
$J_b=J(\boldsymbol\theta^{(b)})$. For nonempty $\mathcal V$,
\begin{equation}
w_b=\frac{\exp(J_b/\lambda)}{\sum_{a\in\mathcal V}\exp(J_a/\lambda)},
\qquad
\boldsymbol\mu^+=\sum_{b\in\mathcal V}w_b\boldsymbol\theta^{(b)}.
\label{eq:offline_sampling_update}
\end{equation}
The temperature $\lambda>0$ controls concentration around
high-scoring samples and is adapted to maintain sample diversity.
A proposal with no feasible samples remains unchanged.
The best feasible candidate is retained until the score plateaus
or the iteration budget is exhausted.

\noindent\emph{Command correction with the refined model.}
The selected M0 tip trajectory $\mathbf r_{\mathrm{tip}}^0(t)$ and
its timing define the fixed execution goal. The original predicted
vehicle path $\mathbf p_q^0(t)$ and position command
$\mathbf p_d^0(t)$ provide secondary penalties that discourage large
changes during correction; the vehicle motion and commands remain
adjustable. Superscript $0$ identifies this plan, distinct from the
earlier shape prior $\mathrm{ref}$.

Figure~\ref{fig:command_correction} illustrates command correction
under the refined M1 model.

Over the original plan's fixed handover interval $[0,T_h]$,
where $0<T_h\leq T$, we minimize
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_{\mathrm{track}}(\boldsymbol\theta)
={}&w_{\mathrm{tip}}\bigl\langle\|\mathbf r_{\mathrm{tip}}-\mathbf r_{\mathrm{tip}}^0\|_2^2\bigr\rangle
+w_q\bigl\langle\|\mathbf p_q-\mathbf p_q^0\|_2^2\bigr\rangle\\
&+w_d\bigl\langle\|\mathbf p_d-\mathbf p_d^0\|_2^2\bigr\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:reference_tracking_cost}
\end{equation}
The states depend on $\boldsymbol\theta$ through the refined
predictor. Brackets average squared distances at matching times,
using prediction samples for the first two terms and command
samples for the third. Positive weights prioritize tip tracking
while limiting vehicle-path and command deviations. Reference
timing, weights, and admissibility constraints remain fixed.

Starting from the latest executed command, regularized
Gauss--Newton updates adjust controls affecting $[0,T_h]$.
Finite differences estimate trajectory sensitivities for a local
least-squares problem with bounded increments and quadratic
damping. Backtracking accepts only steps that reduce
$C_{\mathrm{track}}$ in nonlinear simulation and satisfy
admissibility checks. Iteration stops when no improving step
is found, improvement becomes small, or the budget is exhausted.

The refined model and constraints may not admit exact reproduction
of the fixed reference. A decrease in $C_{\mathrm{track}}$ establishes
improvement in the simulated regularized objective; physical tip
tracking and target approach are evaluated separately in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.
Both stages verify the selected command in a separate rollout
including recovery.

\subsection{Model Refinement from Physical Executions}
\label{subsec:model_refinement}

Starting from the calibrated model $\mathcal M_0$, refinement
produces candidate updates to vehicle response, cable coefficients,
and acceleration corrections. Fitting compares predicted and
measured motion, independently of the target and planned reference.
Cable length, masses, and attachment geometry remain fixed.

Recordings provide executed commands, vehicle pose, and cable-marker
positions. Each fitting window starts from preceding measurements
and advances without resetting states to subsequent observations.
Missing observations are excluded from the loss.

Fitting combines the latest batch, earlier whipping batches,
and preliminary motions with fixed group weights.
Final test recordings remain outside fitting and model selection.

Vehicle fitting uses command-driven predictions. Cable fitting is
driven by measured attachment motion from~\eqref{eq:attachment_mapping},
separating cable error from vehicle-prediction error. Both use
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{data}}
=\sum_{o\in\mathcal O}w_o\,\rho\!\left(\frac{\|\mathbf e_o\|_2^2}{\sigma_o^2}\right),
\label{eq:refinement_data_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal O$ contains valid observations,
$\mathbf e_o\in\mathbb R^3$ is the prediction error, and
$w_o\geq0$ with $\sum_{o\in\mathcal O}w_o=1$.
Position errors are predicted minus measured positions.
Orientation errors are the principal rotation vectors of
$\mathbf R_o^\top\mathbf R_o^{\mathrm{meas}}$.
Positive scales $\sigma_o$ normalize each observation type. The robust penalty
$\rho(z)=2(\sqrt{1+z}-1)$ limits the influence of large errors.
Cable losses emphasize the tip while retaining the other observed
non-attachment markers.

Candidate fitting proceeds in four stages:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Quadrotor response.}
    Fit $\mathbf K_p$, $\mathbf K_v$, $\mathbf K_a$, and
    $\tau_d$ using position errors, keeping the inherited vehicle
    correction fixed. Separately fit $\mathbf S$ and $\tau_R$
    using orientation errors.

    \item \emph{Quadrotor correction.}
    Fix the nominal translation parameters and fit the vehicle
    network using position errors over simulated trajectories.
    Then refit $\mathbf S$ and $\tau_R$: the corrected position
    and velocity predictions alter the nominal acceleration
    used to predict attitude.

    \item \emph{Cable physics.}
    Fit $EI$, $C_b$, and $\gamma_c$ using measured attachment
    motion and marker-position errors, keeping the inherited
    cable correction fixed.

    \item \emph{Cable correction.}
    Fix the fitted cable coefficients and fit the cable network
    using the same measured attachment trajectory and marker loss.
\end{enumerate}

Nominal parameters use fixed bounds and quadratic penalties on
scaled changes from their initial values, with logarithmic
scaling for positive parameters.

The neural stages penalize correction magnitude and deviation
from the inherited correction:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NN}}
={}&\mathcal L_{\mathrm{data}}+\frac{\lambda_a}{3a_{\max}^2}\,
\Bigl\langle\|\Delta\mathbf a\|_2^2\\
&\quad+\|\Delta\mathbf a-\Delta\mathbf a^{\mathrm{prev}}\|_2^2\Bigr\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:refinement_neural_loss}
\end{equation}
Here $\Delta\mathbf a$ denotes the vehicle or free-node cable
correction, and $\Delta\mathbf a^{\mathrm{prev}}$ is the
inherited network evaluated at the same inputs.
Brackets average over weighted fitting windows and times, and
over free nodes for the cable, excluding times without valid
cable observations.
The factor $3a_{\max}^2$ normalizes squared acceleration and
$\lambda_a\geq0$ controls regularization. Training differentiates
through rollouts without measured acceleration labels.

Development recordings select the model used for command
correction; an inherited component may be retained when its
candidate update does not improve prediction. The selected model
is frozen before physical evaluation. Complete command-to-tip
prediction evaluates the assembled model separately from cable
prediction driven by measured attachment motion.

\subsection{Setup and Evaluation}
\label{subsec:experimental_setup}
\label{subsec:experimental_metrics}

The platform uses a 145\,g quadrotor and a 0.9525\,m cable
weighing 17\,g with tracking markers. OptiTrack measures vehicle
pose and cable motion. For the M0 and M1 batches, the vehicle
starts at $(0,0,1.4)$\,m, with target $(1.25,0,1.25)$\,m and
strike direction $+x$.
Commands are executed at 30\,Hz without online cable feedback.
The predictor uses 12 cable nodes and 150\,Hz rollout samples;
the command spline has 12 control points over $T=1.5$\,s.
Correction uses $(w_{\mathrm{tip}},w_q,w_d)=(1,0.1,0.01)$
in~\eqref{eq:reference_tracking_cost}.

The shape prior was manually selected from an earlier MPPI
simulation, and the command seed was the highest-scoring solution
retained in a subsequent development run. Both were developed for
a target 1.25\,m ahead and 0.255\,m below launch, with the same
$+x$ strike direction. The M0/M1 target is 0.15\,m below launch.
These inputs therefore supply prior target-directed behavior.

The initial M0 has no active cable correction. The first update
adds a cable network initialized to zero output, using
$\Delta\mathbf a^{\mathrm{prev}}=\mathbf0$
in~\eqref{eq:refinement_neural_loss}; subsequent updates inherit
its weights. Thus, the M0-to-M1 comparison includes added model
capacity as well as parameter refinement.

We report five flights for each of the M0 and M1 model--command
pairs. The M1 command is corrected toward the original M0
predicted trajectory and timing. In each batch, recordings 1, 2,
and 4 contribute to subsequent fitting; recordings 3 and 5 support
development checks. The selected M2 retains the M1 vehicle network
and updates the nominal vehicle response and cable model, using
the two M1 development recordings for selection.

For physical evaluation of M2, a $10\,\mathrm{cm}\times
10\,\mathrm{cm}$ target is placed at the planned hit position.
The M2 command is executed 20 times. A hit requires physical
contact with the target.

Tip tracking error $E_{\mathrm{tip}}$ is the three-dimensional
RMSE between the measured tip trajectory and the original M0
predicted tip reference at matching timestamps over $[0,T_h]$,
with $T_h=34/30$\,s.
Target metrics are $d_{\min}$ from~\eqref{eq:target_distance}
over $[0,1.5]$\,s and distance $d_\star$ at the original
strike time $t_\star=1.11725$\,s.
For physical evaluation, we substitute the measured trajectory
$\mathbf r_{\mathrm{tip}}^{\mathrm{meas}}$ into~\eqref{eq:target_distance}.
Piecewise-linear interpolation within valid tracking intervals
estimates the minimum; tracking gaps are excluded and reported.
Tip speed is estimated from local position derivatives. Clock alignment uses measured
vehicle streams; missing observations are masked. Statistics
average per-flight metrics, without spatial or target-based
temporal alignment.
